The President then reported that earlier in the year
a donation of 41,000 had been received from the Estate of
Hugo D. Keil. ,

The Vice-President, Mr. Cornwall, commented on the
auditor's comparative statement covering a period of 13 years,
put criticized the method by which the transfer of the Boston
property is shown. Upon motion of Mr. Cornwall, seconded by
Mr. Hutchinson, it was

RESOLVED: That the Auditor be requested to change
the statements to more truly reflect that the transfer of the
Boston property was its return to the heirs of the Estate from
whom it had originally been donated to the Society.

The Vice-President also urged that the Auditor be
instructed to provide the Board with a final statement for 1940
which was delayed owing to the change of auditors this year,
and that copies be given to each member of the Board, in accord-
ance with resolution now in the minute book. The Vice-President
undertook to see that a satisfactory statement was received from
the auditor.

The Secretary reported that a communication had been
received from the American Trust Company enclosing a form of
"Safe-keeping Instructions", which the Society is asked to com-
plete to put in writing the instructions regarding the personal
property held in safe-keeping by the American Trust Company for
the Society. Upon motion of Judge Johnson, seconded by
Dr. Gibbons, it was

RESOLVED: That the President and the Secretary of
this Corporation be and they are hereby authorized on behalf
of this corporation and as its corporate act, to sign a letter
of safe-keeping instructions to American Trust Company in the
form submitted by them, to be dated October 28th, 1941, a copy
of which shall be placed on file.
